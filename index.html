How these scores work

Fatty Liver Index (Bedogni et al., 2006) estimates the chance of fat in the liver from BMI, waist, triglycerides and GGT. Below 30 makes fatty liver unlikely; 60 or above makes it likely; between is uncertain.

FIB-4 (Sterling et al., 2006) estimates the chance of advanced scarring from age, AST, ALT and platelets: age × AST ÷ (platelets × √ALT). Cutoffs follow the AASLD 2023 guidance: below 1.3 is low risk, 1.3 to 2.67 is indeterminate, above 2.67 is high. From age 65 the low-risk cutoff rises to 2.0.

  • FIB-4 is not reliable under age 35, during acute illness, or during a sudden spike in liver enzymes.
  • Neither score diagnoses disease. An amber or red result means a clinician should look further, often with an elastography scan (FibroScan).
  • Both scores were built on specific populations. How well they work in the hospital's patient population still needs checking.
